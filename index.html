01 · The experiment

Two versions, one locked gate

In this game, a gate stops progress until the player waits or pays. New players were randomly given one of two versions: the first gate at level 30 (the original) or at level 40. The main metric is 7-day retention: did the player open the game again 7 days after installing?

Each dot is a level. Dataset: Mobile Games A/B Testing (Kaggle), with one row per player: version, game rounds played in the first 14 days, and day-1 / day-7 retention.

02 · Health checks

Before trusting results, check the test itself

A broken experiment can produce convincing but wrong numbers, so I ran two checks before comparing retention.

⚠️ Flagged

Sample ratio mismatch

49.6% / 50.4%

The split should be 50/50. With 90k players, even this small gap is unlikely to be chance (chi-square p = 0.0086). The cause can't be found from this data, and the gap is small, so I continue but report it as a limitation. In a real team this goes to data engineering first.

✓ Passed

Removing players who never played

3,994 players removed

Players with 0 rounds never reached any gate, so the gate couldn't affect them. Both groups lose a similar share (4.3% vs 4.5%, p = 0.17), so removing them doesn't favour either side. 86,195 players remain.

The statistics

MetricGate 30Gate 40Differencezp-value95% CI (pp)

Two-sided two-proportion z-test, α = 0.05. With about 43,000 players per group, the test had ~85% power to detect the observed day-7 gap, and the smallest gap it can reliably detect is about 0.76 pp. The day-1 gap is below the ~0.96 pp it can detect for that metric, so day 1 means "too small to tell", not "no effect".

05 · Bayesian view

How sure can we be?

A p-value says how surprising the data would be if nothing changed. A Bayesian model answers the more natural question: what is the probability gate 30 is actually better? Each version's true retention rate gets a Beta posterior, starting from a flat prior.

06 · Did players quit at the wall?

Same amount of play, different return rate

If gate 40 made players quit at the wall, fewer gate-40 players would get past round 40. They don't: the curves for share of players who played at least k rounds are nearly identical. The gate changes whether players come back, not visibly how much they play.

08 · Verdict

Keep the gate at level 30

🔒

Small effect, consistent signal

Gate 30 retains about 4% more players at day 7. The z-test, the confidence interval and the Bayesian model all agree, and the experiment was large enough to detect an effect of this size. Moving the gate to level 40 would quietly lose players every day.

Limitations

  • Sample ratio mismatch (49.6% / 50.4%). The cause should be investigated before acting.
  • Revenue numbers are illustrative. Real install volume and player LTV are needed.
  • Only day-1 and day-7 retention exist. Long-term retention, purchases and novelty effects are unmeasured.
  • Rounds played aren't levels reached, so the exact quit point can't be seen.

Methods

  • Data checks: duplicates, missing values, a 49,854-round logging outlier.
  • Sample-ratio test, then modified intention-to-treat (players with ≥ 1 round).
  • Two-proportion z-test, Wald confidence intervals, power analysis.
  • Beta-Binomial Bayesian model with a flat prior.
  • Python · pandas · SciPy · statsmodels · seaborn. This page has no dependencies: all statistics are recomputed in the browser from the raw counts.
